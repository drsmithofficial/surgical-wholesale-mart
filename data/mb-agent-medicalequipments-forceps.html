Frequently Asked Questions

Forceps are surgical instruments used to grasp, hold, or manipulate tissues and objects during medical procedures. They are commonly used in surgeries, childbirth, and dental treatments for precision handling.

Forceps are used in pregnancy to assist with the delivery of a baby, particularly in cases of prolonged labor or when the baby needs help to be delivered safely. They help guide the baby out of the birth canal without causing harm to the mother or child.

Another name for forceps is "tongs," especially when referring to instruments used for gripping or handling objects. In medical contexts, they may also be called "surgical forceps" or "grasping forceps" based on their specific function.

Forceps are used on a baby during delivery to help guide the baby's head out of the birth canal when there are complications, such as prolonged labor or fetal distress. They are designed to gently grasp the baby's head and assist with a safer, quicker birth.

Forceps are used to grasp, hold, or manipulate tissues during medical procedures, ensuring precise control in surgeries or childbirth. In obstetrics, they assist in guiding the baby's head during difficult deliveries.

Forceps are handheld, hinged instruments used primarily in medical and surgical procedures to grasp, hold, or manipulate tissues and other materials. They function similarly to tongs or tweezers and are essential tools in nearly every clinical and surgical setting. Their design allows for precision, control, and minimal trauma during procedures.

 

Types of Forceps (Categorized by Function/Specialty)

Surgical Forceps

Used during surgeries to handle tissues or dressings. They are further divided into:

  • Tissue Forceps: e.g., toothed forceps for gripping tough tissues.
  • Dressing Forceps: For handling gauze or dressing materials.

Obstetric Forceps

Specialized for assisting in childbirth by guiding the baby's head through the birth canal when necessary.

Dental Forceps

Designed for tooth extraction and manipulation of oral tissues during dental procedures.

Dissecting Forceps

Utilized during dissection to hold or manipulate delicate tissues without causing damage.

Hemostatic Forceps

These clamp blood vessels to stop or control bleeding during surgeries (e.g., mosquito forceps, Kelly forceps).

Thumb Forceps

Tweezer-like forceps used in both surgical and non-surgical settings for grasping.

Entomological or Laboratory Forceps

Used for handling small specimens or materials during lab work.

Artery Forceps

Artery forceps are used to clamp blood vessels, particularly in vascular surgeries, to prevent blood flow during operations.

Needle Forceps

Needle forceps are used for holding and manipulating needles during suturing in surgical procedures, ensuring precise placement of stitches.

 

Uses of Forceps

  • Grasping and holding tissues during surgery
  • Removing foreign objects
  • Applying or removing dressings
  • Assisting in childbirth
  • Controlling bleeding by clamping vessels
  • Extracting teeth in dental procedures
  • Handling delicate materials in laboratories

 

Proper Handling and Care of Forceps

  • Clean and sterilize forceps before and after every use using appropriate disinfection methods (autoclaving is common for surgical-grade forceps).
  • Inspect regularly for signs of wear, rust, or misalignment.
  • Store properly in sterile containers or trays to prevent contamination and damage.
  • Handle gently during use to avoid bending tips or misaligning jaws.

 

How to Choose the Right Forceps?

  • Choose based on the procedure—surgical, dental, obstetric, or laboratory.
  • Use toothed forceps for firmer grip on tough tissue, smooth tips for delicate tissues.
  • Stainless steel is preferred for its durability and ease of sterilization.
  • Comfortable grip and precise control reduce hand fatigue during lengthy procedures.
